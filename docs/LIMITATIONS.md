# Limitations (read before quoting any number)

1. **Synthetic data.** Until the organizer dataset is loaded, every headline number comes from a seeded synthetic dataset. Many of its batches are close to expiry by design (remaining life as low as 22 % of base). This makes spoilage-aware planning matter more than it might on a real distributor's stock. Results are not real-world performance.
2. **Placeholder physics.** Q10 values, chill thresholds, minimum sellable life and handling losses are uncalibrated placeholders. The ranking of decisions is more trustworthy than the absolute spoilage percentages.
3. **The planner's model is also the simulator's model.** The planner and the simulator share the shelf-life physics, with independent random draws. Misspecified noise levels are tested in the sensitivity analysis, but a structurally wrong physics model would affect both. Organizer hidden scenarios are the real test.
4. **Baseline strength.** FIFO and greedy-by-price may not divert and use point estimates by definition. Part of the planner's spoilage advantage comes from diverting near-expiry stock to salvage, roughly 32 t per cycle on the synthetic data. For that reason `spoiled_or_diverted_pct` is reported next to spoilage, and the deterministic optimizer ablation can also divert.
5. **One dispatch cycle.** The planner decides one cycle at a time. Hold is valued with a carry-over rule (80 % of purchase value if still sellable) rather than a multi-day lookahead. Net revenue without carry-over is reported too.
6. **Demand.** Elasticity is linear, at 1.5. Demand at a market is independent of other markets' prices. The plan caps sellable shipments at forecast demand and does not hedge for demand uncertainty. Unsold stock is carried or spoiled.
7. **Delivery windows** are measured in hours after departure. Changing the departure hour does not move market opening times.
8. **Live mode.** OSRM's public demo server gives car-profile routes without traffic, scaled by a 1.35 truck factor. Open-Meteo gives forecasts for 4 waypoints per route. Prices are replayed, not live. Live mode is for demonstration and is not part of the scored evaluation.
9. **Fulfillment ceiling.** On the synthetic data, lane capacity and short remaining life limit what can reach some markets. Demand fulfillment stays around 60 % even for the planner.
10. **Statistics.** Confidence intervals use a normal approximation of paired differences across scenarios. Scenarios are generated by our own scenario model, not by the organizers' hidden scenarios.
